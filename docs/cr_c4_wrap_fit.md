## 1. What the renderer actually does (measured)

### 1.1 The engine does NOT mirror: stored order is screen order

`docs/tests.md` (C1, "Big finding 1") says the engine mirrors every loc
value. The pixels say otherwise. Rendering the stored codepoints left to
right reproduces the screenshot glyph for glyph; rendering them right to
left produces a mirror image that matches nothing (`logs/crops/C4/*_asis.png`
vs `*_rev.png`). Quantitatively, best mask symmetric difference over
(m, k, space, y, x) on the five drawn ERE lines:

| font | stored order | reversed (mirror) |
|---|---|---|
| `la_gioconda_12.cuf` | symdiff 13633 | symdiff 14206 |
| `georgia_italic_12.cuf` | symdiff 13614 | symdiff 14288 |
| **best** | **13614** | **14206** |

The stored order wins by 592 pixels (4.2 % fewer).

The mistaken reading came from joining words that had already been
reversed by hand: the *words* in the value are in reading (logical) order,
so reversing the codepoint stream of a line makes each word read backwards
— which is what the C1 note interpreted as the engine's mirroring.

A purely semantic check was also attempted — scoring each word against the
Arabic Presentation-Forms-B shape rules (in visual left-to-right order a
joined run must go final/isolated -> medial* -> initial) — but it is not
conclusive here: the stored strings carry U+0640 padding and punctuation,
which are neutral in those rules, and the neutral handling swamps the
signal (196 violations in stored order against 272 reversed, on 220
words). It is mentioned for completeness only; the pixel evidence above is
the load-bearing proof.

**Consequence for authoring:** a `\n` is authored on the same side the
reader sees. There is no flip to compensate for, and the old 'wrong side'
diagnosis of the orphan words is not the cause of them (section 1.2).

`ratio spread` = max-min of `measured / sum(aw)`; `residual stdev` =
standard deviation of `measured - sum(aw)` over the same lines.

**Neither statistic identifies the font uniquely.** Six measurements cannot
identify three free parameters (`n_chars` is nearly proportional to
`sum(aw)`, so `m` and `k` trade off), and about ten fonts land within 5 % of
each other on both statistics: `georgia_italic_14` 4.43, `la_gioconda_16`
4.52, `arialunicode_12` 4.58, `la_gioconda_12` 4.61. The font is therefore
chosen on the two measurements that *do* separate it:

* glyph size: screen alef stems are 3 px of ink wide and 10-11 px tall, and
  the bek stems 8-9 px tall, matching `georgia_italic_14` (`inkw=3`,
  `inkh=11`) and excluding `georgia_italic_16` (`inkw=4`) and
  `georgia_italic_12` (`inkh=9`);
* the mask-overlay fit of 1.1-1.2 reaches its best overlap on the
  size-12-to-16 group and none of the larger sizes.

`la_gioconda_16` is not separable from `georgia_italic_14` by these tests
(identical metrics, different outlines); `georgia_italic_14` is adopted
because it sits first on the residual statistic.

### 1.4 The adopted model

`georgia_italic_14` (`v_size` 23, `line_height` 14, `baseline` 19), fitted
with `m = 0.9559`, `k = -0.1146`, `space = 3.810`. What the fit is worth:

* The near-1.0 `m` and near-zero `k` mean the engine advances each Arabic
  codepoint by approximately the CUF `aw` at this surface, plus a small
  additive term of about +6 px per line.
* It is **not** a unique identification (see 1.3) and it is **not** exact:
  the two lines whose stored values carry heavy U+0640 padding come out
  45 px and 28 px too wide.
* What it is good for is choosing word boundaries with a wide margin, which
  is exactly what section 2 uses it for.

needs the *difference* between two candidate fits, so an honest bound on
the model's wrap accuracy is rms 14 px, worst case 45 px, on ~300 px lines

**Wrap-fit criterion: PARTIAL.** The model tracks the measured lines to
+-5 px on the clean ones and is out by up to 45 px on the two padded
ones, so it does **not** reproduce the engine's own break positions to
pixel accuracy. It is good enough to choose word boundaries that fit
the box with a large safety margin, which is what section 2 uses it for,
but the strict reading of the criterion ("the line-break offsets must
match the screenshots") is only met on the lines the engine already
drew, seen below, not in general.

## 3. Right-alignment test plan (planned, NOT built)

Variants:

* **A — no padding.** Text starts at the box's left margin, as today.
* **B — NBSP padding**, computed so each line's drawn right edge reaches
  the box's right edge.

### 3.1 Where the pad has to go (verified against the renderer)

The renderer draws the stored order left to right. A U+00A0 is an
ink-free advance, so:

* a pad **appended** to the stored line adds empty advance at the END of
  the drawn line, which moves the visible ink **left** (away from the
  right edge) and leaves a gap on the right;
* a pad **prepended** to the stored line adds empty advance at the START
  of the drawn line, which moves the visible ink **right**, up to the
  right edge.

So variant B is `line_new = "\u00a0" * n + line`, authored at the START
of each stored line.

The task's instruction — "in logical order a leading pad becomes a
trailing pad on screen (RTL), so the padding must be AUTHORED at the END
of each stored line to push text right when mirrored" — is **correct only
under the mirror hypothesis**, which §1.1 refutes. Under the measured
model it is inverted: authoring the pad at the END gives variant **Bt**,
which pushes the ink left, producing a left-aligned block with a growing
gap on the right — the opposite of the goal. Conclusion: author the pad at
the **START** of each stored line.

same counts would move the ink *left* by N x 4.66 px instead, leaving a
right-hand gap of up to 173 px on the longest line --
visibly not right-aligned.

## 5. What was NOT achieved

1. **The strict wrap-fit criterion does not pass.** The engine's exact
   per-codepoint advance table is not identified. The adopted 3-parameter
   model reproduces the clean ERE lines to +-5 px, but two of the eight
   visible lines are out by +45 px and +28 px, so it does not reproduce
   the engine's break positions to pixel accuracy in general. What it does
   reproduce exactly is the *content* of every drawn line -- the engine
   honours our hard newlines, so the break offsets in section 1.5 ARE the
   screenshot offsets.
2. **The model cannot be used to predict an engine break it has not seen**
   to better than about 15 % of the box width. That is why the recomputed
   wraps keep the original line count and only move word boundaries, and
   why the budget carries a hard ceiling instead of trusting the target.
3. **A per-codepoint recovery exists but is unusable.** `cr_adv_solve.py`
   fits all 14 measured lines to 2.34 px rms, but the system is
   underdetermined (14 equations, 64 codepoints) and the ridge solution
   returns unphysical advances (U+FE8D = -0.72 px, U+0640 = 12.57 px).
   It is therefore not used to author wraps.
4. **The task's two structural premises are refuted by the pixels:**
   the engine does not mirror (section 1.1) and it does not re-wrap
   (section 1.2). The wraps are still recomputed as asked, in logical
   order, with no word split and no line over the ceiling -- but the
   engine would have honoured the old ones just as literally. The real
   defect the C1 screenshots show is the decorative U+0640 padding sitting
   at the wrong end of each line plus the orphan words it creates; the C4
   values drop the padding and re-balance the words.
5. **Some recomputed lines stay above the 319 px target** (up to 340 px):
   the two WRE values and both `long_description` values keep 2-4 such
   lines, because their fixed line budget cannot hold the content under the
   target given their word lengths. They stay under the 345 px hard ceiling,
   which is the widest line the engine demonstrably drew in this panel. The
   two `start_pos_factions_description_*` values -- the ones the C1
   screenshots actually show -- fit under the target on every line.
